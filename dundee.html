MyDundee and Registry

The source files show two core system families: MyDundee for learning and assessments, and registry or eVision workflows for applications, records, and official student administration.

Where each system lives

  • MyDundee: module pages, learning resources, submissions, extra English materials, and online Listening, Reading, and Writing exams.
  • eVision: registry-facing actions, records, and official letters.
  • Dundee Students site: central services and official guidance.

Use the module descriptions on this page as the quick reference for what each platform is used for. The only global jump button is placed at the bottom of the page.

How to use MyDundee well

  • Check each module space early because project work often expects study beyond the lecture introduction.

Semester 1 Modules

The first-semester notes describe rotating project work, English transition support, and subject teaching that expects fast adaptation. The cards below focus on content, assessment style, and what usually helps students pass or perform safely.

Project Structure

How the rotation works

Students usually complete four short projects in Computing, Mathematics, Mechanical, and Civil topics. Groups are random, usually 5 to 8 students, and peer review can affect individual marks.

  • Projects are used to introduce Dundee teaching style and teamwork.
  • Different degree cohorts may be mixed in the same class.
  • English communication matters inside project marking, not only technical output.
Computing

Processing, drawing tools, and demonstration

The Computing project introduces programming through Processing. Students build a simple drawing tool and explain it in a demonstration.

  • Main output: group drawing software plus demo.
  • How to stay on track: do not wait for class alone, because the final product needs more than the starter examples.
  • Useful habit: practice extra features early and read the recommended MyDundee materials.
Mathematics

Game design and academic report

The Mathematics project focuses on designing a game, identifying a mathematical problem inside it, and writing an academic-style report.

  • Main output: group-designed game plus written report.
  • How to stay on track: treat report writing seriously because academic style matters heavily.
  • Critical warning: if only one student should submit, do not let multiple group members upload the same file to Turnitin.
Mechanical

Fan design, testing, and poster

This project covers Excel data handling, fan design, risk awareness, testing with equipment, and turning the results into a poster-style report.

  • Main output: built fan model, measured data, poster report, and oral questioning.
  • How to stay on track: take part in the whole build and testing process because final review can include individual questioning.
  • Common issue: poor time or materials planning makes the final stage much more stressful.
Civil

CAD bridge design, testing, report, and presentation

The Civil project introduces basic CAD drawing, materials, and bridge knowledge. Students then design and build a bridge, test its load capacity, analyse the result, and present the project as a group.

  • Main output: CAD design, physical model, test results, report, and presentation.
  • How to stay on track: understand the structure before building and plan material use carefully.
  • Performance tip: this project usually feels clearer when the team assigns roles early.
English for Academic Skills I

Academic English, presentation, and four-skill exams

This module helps students adapt to English-medium study through referencing, presentation technique, and general writing, listening, speaking, and reading development.

  • Main assessments: coursework plus final Writing, Listening, Reading, and Speaking components.
  • How to stay on track: practise speaking delivery, not only content, because confidence and presentation quality matter.
  • Support tip: use MyDundee extra materials and English corner sessions regularly.
Calculus for Science and Engineering

Worksheets, class test, and final exam

The Calculus notes describe two main assessments: a class test and a final exam. Teaching staff may vary, so students benefit from relying on the prepared learning sheets rather than one teaching style.

  • Main assessments: class test plus final exam.
  • How to stay on track: preview worksheets before class and learn mathematics vocabulary in English.
  • Practical benefit: pre-reading reduces pressure when teaching pace, accent, or handwriting changes.
Study Routine

Habits that protect your mark

The notes also include practical study advice: use libraries and available study spaces, review materials early, and treat project teamwork as a real grading factor rather than an optional extra.

  • Join every important stage of group work because final questioning often exposes weak participation.
  • Use spare time to build technical confidence before the final submission rush.
  • Keep your English exposure daily, especially for subject vocabulary and presentations.

Exam Rules

The DIICSU exam documents are strict. They cover room behaviour, late arrival, allowed equipment, online platform rules, and academic misconduct triggers. Many serious penalties come from avoidable mistakes rather than weak academic performance.

General exam-room rules

  • Phones must be switched off and stored in bags at the front of the room.
  • Smart watches must also be switched off and stored away. Keeping one on your person counts as misconduct.
  • Enter in silence, remain silent, and do not start until instructed.
  • Bring your University of Dundee ID card and place it on the desk.

Leaving, lateness, and timing

  • You cannot leave an exam that lasts less than one hour.
  • For longer exams, students may leave only after 30 minutes and not in the last 15 minutes.
  • Late entry is allowed only if no student has left yet, and there is no extra time.
  • Emergency exits normally mean you cannot re-enter.

Online English exam setup

  • Listening, Reading, and Writing exams are run on MyDundee.
  • Bring a charged laptop because no replacement equipment is promised.
  • Keep devices closed until told to log in.
  • Keep only one tab open: MyDundee.
  • For Listening, bring working headphones and use the note paper provided.

Allowed equipment

  • Three black or blue pens.
  • A ruler.
  • A scientific calculator that is not a graphing calculator.
  • Pens or pencils for Listening and Reading where permitted.
  • No personal paper brought into the exam room.
High-risk mistakes: keeping extra browser tabs open during a MyDundee exam, using translation or auto-translation software, leaving non-English interface text visible when English-only setup is required, or carrying a phone or smart watch on your person. The documents frame these as academic misconduct, not minor errors.

Referencing and Citation

The citation notes explain referencing as both an academic requirement and a misconduct safeguard. The programme uses Harvard style, which means in-text citation plus a reference list at the end.

When you must reference

  • When text or ideas are copied directly from a website, book, or journal.
  • When you paraphrase or combine other people's ideas.
  • When you reuse your own previously written or published work.

The notes explicitly connect poor referencing to plagiarism risk and warn that large-scale AI writing can also be treated as academic misconduct.

What Harvard style includes

  • In-text citation: author plus year inside the paragraph where the source is used.
  • Reference list: full source details at the end, normally sorted alphabetically.
  • The reference list includes much more publication detail than the in-text citation.

Tools mentioned in the source notes

  • Library or database citation generators.
  • Mendeley for organising references and inserting citations into Word.
  • Turnitin Draft Coach for checking similarity, citations, and grammar before submission.
Mendeley Harvard guide Cite Them Right

Practical advice for safe submissions

  • Keep a source list as you read instead of rebuilding it at the end.
  • Reference paraphrases, not just direct quotations.
  • Check submission rules before using Turnitin, especially in group work.
  • Use support tools to improve your own writing, not replace it.

Mitigating Circumstances

If illness, personal emergencies, or other serious situations significantly affect performance, the DIICSU form explains how to request formal consideration.

Where to send the form

Submit the application by email, with supporting evidence attached, to SSEN-DIICSU@dundee.ac.uk.

The email subject should include: Mitigating Circumstances - Private and Confidential.

What the form asks for

  • Name, phone number, Dundee email, student number, programme, year of study, and adviser.
  • Which modules or exams were affected.
  • Assessment titles, dates, and submission status.
  • Clear explanation of the circumstances and their impact.
  • Dates showing the period affected and evidence attached.

Timing and consent

  • The form notes that deadlines are set near the end of each semester and communicated in advance.
  • You are asked to consent to discussion with your Student Adviser.
  • You also declare that the information is correct and can be used by the committee.

When to use this route

  • Use it for genuine serious impact, not for normal deadline pressure.
  • If poor equipment stops you taking an online exam, the rules state you may need this process and a retake.
  • Evidence matters, so collect documents early.
